# RETRIEVAL PIPELINE

---

## Overview

The Compliance & Risk Intelligence Systems (CRIS) framework is intended to support structured retrieval workflows across:

- regulatory systems,
- spatial-validation infrastructure,
- environmental planning systems,
- workflow-intelligence layers,
- and operational planning-support architectures.

This document defines the conceptual retrieval-pipeline architecture direction for future CRIS infrastructure development.

---

# Retrieval Infrastructure Position

CRIS treats retrieval pipelines as:

> operational planning-intelligence coordination workflows.

The framework prioritizes:

- semantic traceability,
- structured interpretation,
- operational readability,
- modular scalability,
- and workflow coordination visibility.

The objective is to support interpretable retrieval systems rather than opaque automation infrastructure.

---

# Core Retrieval Stages

Future CRIS retrieval workflows may include:

1. Document Ingestion
2. Parsing & Extraction
3. Semantic Chunking
4. Metadata Structuring
5. Embedding Generation
6. Vector Storage
7. Retrieval Coordination
8. Interpretation Support
9. Operational Output Generation

Each stage functions within broader planning-intelligence workflows.

---

# Stage 1 — Document Ingestion

Future ingestion workflows may support:

- zoning documents,
- building bylaws,
- development control regulations,
- environmental planning reports,
- workflow documentation,
- occupancy standards,
- and operational planning records.

The objective is to structure planning information into operationally interpretable systems.

# Retrieval Modularity Principle

All retrieval systems within CRIS are intended to remain:

- modular,
- independently scalable,
- operationally interpretable,
- and adaptable across jurisdictions and project types.

This allows future infrastructure expansion without destabilizing foundational methodology.
