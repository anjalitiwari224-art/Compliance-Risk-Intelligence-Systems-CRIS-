# TERMINOLOGY

---

## Overview

This document establishes the operational terminology used within the Compliance & Risk Intelligence Systems (CRIS) framework.

The objective is to maintain:

- methodological consistency,
- infrastructure clarity,
- operational readability,
- and conceptual stability across repository development.

---

## Compliance Intelligence

Within CRIS, "compliance intelligence" refers to:

> structured operational analysis and interpretation support related to regulatory, environmental, spatial, and workflow systems within built environments.

The term does not imply autonomous legal authority or statutory approval capability.

---

## Regulatory Intelligence

"Regulatory intelligence" refers to:

- zoning interpretation,
- bylaw structuring,
- approval workflow organization,
- development control analysis,
- and regulatory coordination systems.

The framework assists interpretation workflows but does not replace governing authorities.

---

## Spatial Validation

"Spatial validation" refers to:

- circulation analysis,
- occupancy coordination,
- movement-system evaluation,
- bottleneck identification,
- egress assessment,
- and operational compatibility review.

The term does not imply psychological prediction or neuroscientific interpretation.

---

## Environmental Intelligence

"Environmental intelligence" refers to:

- daylight coordination,
- passive ventilation awareness,
- climatic adaptation support,
- flood-risk consideration,
- and environmental planning systems.

Environmental analysis within CRIS is operational and planning-oriented rather than engineering-grade simulation infrastructure.

---

## Operational Systems

"Operational systems" refers to:

- circulation workflows,
- occupancy systems,
- approval coordination,
- environmental interaction,
- and built-environment functionality structures.

The framework prioritizes operational readability and coordination clarity.

---

## Workflow Intelligence

"Workflow intelligence" refers to:

- approval sequencing,
- dependency mapping,
- procedural coordination,
- bottleneck analysis,
- and operational planning workflows.

The framework supports workflow interpretation rather than legal automation systems.

---

## Risk Intelligence

"Risk intelligence" refers to:

- conflict identification,
- approval-risk awareness,
- environmental incompatibility detection,
- workflow coordination risks,
- and operational planning vulnerabilities.

The framework supports early-stage identification and structured interpretation.

---

## AI-Assisted Infrastructure

Within CRIS, "AI-assisted" refers to:

- assistive analysis systems,
- workflow organization support,
- pattern structuring,
- regulation parsing,
- and operational interpretation assistance.

AI is not positioned as:

- autonomous authority,
- legal governance infrastructure,
- engineering replacement,
- or statutory certification systems.

---

## Built-Environment Intelligence Infrastructure

Within CRIS, built-environment intelligence infrastructure refers to:

> interconnected regulatory, environmental, spatial, operational, and workflow systems supporting planning coordination and structured analysis across built environments.

The framework treats architecture and planning as operational systems rather than isolated design outputs.

---

## Institutional Readability

"Institutional readability" refers to:

- operational clarity,
- structured information hierarchy,
- procedural transparency,
- and professional communication standards appropriate for planning and regulatory systems.

The framework prioritizes clarity over technological spectacle.

---

## Methodological Restraint

"Methodological restraint" refers to:

- avoiding exaggerated claims,
- avoiding unsupported predictive narratives,
- avoiding pseudo-scientific positioning,
- and maintaining operationally defensible system definitions.

The framework prioritizes credibility and scalable infrastructure development.

---

## Development Position

This terminology framework may evolve progressively as CRIS infrastructure systems, workflow methodologies, and planning intelligence architectures expand across future repository stages.
