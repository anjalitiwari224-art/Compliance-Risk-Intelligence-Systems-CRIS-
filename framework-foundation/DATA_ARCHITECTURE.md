# DATA ARCHITECTURE

---

## Overview

The Compliance & Risk Intelligence Systems (CRIS) framework is intended to support structured operational intelligence workflows across:

- regulatory systems,
- spatial systems,
- environmental planning systems,
- workflow coordination structures,
- and risk-analysis infrastructure.

This document defines the conceptual data architecture direction of the CRIS framework.

---

# Data Infrastructure Position

CRIS treats data as:

> structured operational planning intelligence inputs.

The framework prioritizes:

- modular organization,
- operational readability,
- workflow traceability,
- and scalable infrastructure coordination.

The objective is to support interpretable planning intelligence systems rather than opaque automation architectures.

---

# Primary Data Categories

Future CRIS infrastructure may organize data across categories including:

1. Regulatory Data
2. Spatial Data
3. Environmental Data
4. Workflow Data
5. Risk-Analysis Data
6. Visualization Data
7. Operational Metadata

Each category functions as part of broader planning intelligence workflows.

---

# Regulatory Data

Regulatory data may include:

- zoning classifications,
- building bylaws,
- development control regulations,
- occupancy standards,
- fire-safety requirements,
- accessibility conditions,
- and approval workflow rules.

The framework supports operational organization and interpretation assistance.

---

# Spatial Data

Spatial data may include:

- circulation layouts,
- occupancy distribution structures,
- movement pathways,
- egress systems,
- operational zoning systems,
- and spatial compatibility conditions.

Spatial systems are intended to support operational validation workflows.

---

# Environmental Data

Environmental data may include:

- daylight conditions,
- ventilation relationships,
- climatic exposure systems,
- flood-risk indicators,
- thermal coordination layers,
- and environmental buffering conditions.

Environmental systems are integrated into broader planning coordination infrastructure.

---

# Workflow Data

Workflow systems may include:

- approval sequences,
- validation dependencies,
- procedural coordination structures,
- workflow bottleneck mapping,
- and operational planning timelines.

Workflow data supports structured planning coordination and operational visibility.

---

# Risk-Analysis Data

Risk-analysis systems may organize:

- approval-risk indicators,
- regulatory conflicts,
- environmental incompatibilities,
- circulation vulnerabilities,
- occupancy stress conditions,
- and operational planning risks.

The objective is to support early-stage interpretation and planning awareness.

---

# Visualization Data

Visualization systems may organize:

- zoning overlays,
- compliance matrices,
- environmental maps,
- workflow diagrams,
- circulation visualizations,
- and operational dashboard systems.

Visualization infrastructure prioritizes institutional readability and operational clarity.

---

# Operational Metadata

Operational metadata may include:

- project classifications,
- jurisdiction references,
- workflow relationships,
- infrastructure dependencies,
- operational tags,
- and system-level coordination structures.

Metadata systems support traceability and scalable infrastructure organization.

---

# AI-Assisted Data Role

Within CRIS, AI may assist in:

- regulation parsing,
- classification workflows,
- pattern organization,
- dependency mapping,
- environmental interpretation support,
- and operational planning analysis.

AI functions only as an assistive infrastructure layer within broader planning intelligence systems.

---

# Data Modularity Principle

All CRIS data systems are intended to remain:

- modular,
- independently scalable,
- operationally interpretable,
- and adaptable across jurisdictions and project types.

This allows future infrastructure growth without destabilizing foundational architecture.

---

# Infrastructure Direction

Future implementation systems may include:

- structured retrieval infrastructure,
- workflow coordination systems,

- and scalable planning-support platforms.

Implementation infrastructure may evolve separately from the public repository framework.

---

# Institutional Position

CRIS prioritizes:

- operational clarity,
- data traceability,
- scalable infrastructure coordination,
