# SYSTEM TAXONOMY

---

## Overview

The Compliance & Risk Intelligence Systems (CRIS) framework is structured as a modular built-environment intelligence infrastructure composed of interconnected operational systems.

This taxonomy defines the primary infrastructure layers and their functional relationships across the CRIS framework.

---

# Primary Infrastructure Layers

The CRIS framework is organized into the following core systems:

1. Regulatory Intelligence Systems
2. Spatial Validation Systems
3. Environmental Intelligence Systems
4. Risk Analysis Systems
5. Approval Workflow Systems
6. Visualization Systems
7. Research & Methodology Systems

Each system functions independently while remaining interoperable within broader planning and coordination workflows.

---

# Regulatory Intelligence Systems

Regulatory intelligence systems include:

- zoning interpretation,
- bylaw structuring,
- development control analysis,
- approval dependency mapping,
- compliance classification,
- and workflow coordination systems.

These systems support operational regulatory interpretation workflows across planning environments.

---

# Spatial Validation Systems

Spatial validation systems include:

- circulation analysis,
- occupancy coordination,
- movement intelligence,
- bottleneck detection,
- egress validation,
- and operational compatibility review.

Spatial systems focus on operational planning functionality rather than aesthetic evaluation alone.

---

# Environmental Intelligence Systems

Environmental intelligence systems include:

- daylight coordination,
- passive ventilation assessment,
- climatic adaptation workflows,
- flood-risk awareness,
- thermal buffering logic,
- and environmental planning systems.

Environmental systems operate as integrated planning intelligence layers.

---

# Risk Analysis Systems

Risk-analysis systems include:

- approval-risk categorization,
- conflict identification,
- environmental incompatibility analysis,
- circulation-risk assessment,
- workflow bottleneck detection,
- and operational vulnerability mapping.

The objective is to support early-stage planning awareness and coordination clarity.

---

# Approval Workflow Systems

Approval workflow systems include:

- procedural sequencing,
- validation dependencies,
- coordination mapping,
- interdepartmental workflows,
- operational process structures,
- and planning intelligence systems.

These systems support structured workflow coordination before formal submission stages.

---

# Visualization Systems

Visualization systems include:

- zoning overlays,
- compliance matrices,
- circulation diagrams,
- environmental mapping systems,
- workflow dashboards,
- and operational intelligence interfaces.

Visualization infrastructure prioritizes institutional readability and operational clarity.

---

# Research & Methodology Systems

Research systems include:

- methodological frameworks,
- operational terminology,
- planning intelligence structures,
- environmental coordination methodologies,
- and infrastructure-definition systems.

Research systems support long-term conceptual stability and infrastructure scalability.

---

# Cross-System Relationships

The CRIS framework treats all infrastructure layers as interconnected operational systems.

Examples include:

- environmental systems influencing spatial validation,
- zoning systems affecting workflow dependencies,
- circulation systems affecting occupancy risks,
- and approval workflows interacting with regulatory coordination structures.

The framework prioritizes systems-level interpretation rather than isolated component analysis.

---

# AI-Assisted Infrastructure Layer

AI within CRIS may support:

- regulation parsing,
- workflow organization,
- environmental interpretation assistance,
- conflict clustering,
- pattern structuring,
- and operational analysis support.

AI functions only as an assistive coordination layer within broader infrastructure systems.

---

# Modularity Principle

All CRIS systems are intended to remain:

- modular,
- independently scalable,
- operationally structured,
- and adaptable across jurisdictions and project types.

This allows future infrastructure expansion without destabilizing core methodological architecture.

---

# Institutional Position

CRIS prioritizes:

- structured infrastructure design,
- operational clarity,
- scalable intelligence systems,
- institutional readability,
- and methodological restraint.

The framework intentionally avoids speculative technological positioning and unsupported automation narratives.

---

# Development Status

The CRIS taxonomy framework remains under active development.

Additional infrastructure layers, coordination systems, and operational intelligence modules may evolve progressively across future repository stages.
