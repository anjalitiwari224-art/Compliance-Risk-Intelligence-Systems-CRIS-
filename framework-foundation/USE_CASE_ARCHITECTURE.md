# USE CASE ARCHITECTURE

---

## Overview

The Compliance & Risk Intelligence Systems (CRIS) framework is intended to support operational planning-intelligence workflows across multiple built-environment contexts.

This document defines the conceptual use-case architecture direction of the CRIS framework.

---

# Use-Case Infrastructure Position

CRIS treats use cases as:

> operational planning-coordination environments requiring structured intelligence support systems.

The framework prioritizes:

- regulatory coordination,
- environmental awareness,
- spatial validation,
- workflow visibility,
- and operational planning clarity.

The objective is to support scalable planning-intelligence infrastructure across diverse project environments.

---

# Primary Use-Case Categories

Future CRIS systems may support workflows across:

1. Architectural Planning
2. Urban & Municipal Planning
3. Mixed-Use Developments
4. Hospitality Infrastructure
5. High-Density Residential Systems
6. Institutional Environments
7. Infrastructure Coordination Systems
8. Environmental Planning Systems

Each use case may interact with broader operational planning-intelligence workflows.

---

# Architectural Planning Systems

Architectural workflows may support:

- zoning interpretation,
- circulation validation,
- occupancy coordination,
- environmental awareness,
- approval workflow visibility,
- and operational planning review.

The framework supports planning coordination rather than formal statutory certification.

---

# Urban & Municipal Planning

Municipal workflows may support:

- workflow organization,
- environmental coordination,

The framework is intended to support interpretation assistance rather than municipal governance replacement.

---

# AI-Assisted Use-Case Role

Within CRIS, AI may assist in:

- workflow interpretation,
- operational analysis,
- environmental coordination support,
- dependency mapping,
- pattern organization,
- and planning-intelligence assistance.

AI is not positioned as:

- autonomous planning authority,
- statutory governance infrastructure,
- engineering certification systems,
- or legal approval intelligence.

---

# Use-Case Modularity Principle

All CRIS use-case systems are intended to remain:

- modular,
- independently scalable,
- operationally interpretable,
- and adaptable across jurisdictions and project types.

This allows future infrastructure expansion without destabilizing foundational methodology.

---

# Institutional Position

CRIS prioritizes:

- operational clarity,
- structured planning intelligence,
- scalable infrastructure coordination,
- institutional readability,
- and methodological restraint.

The framework intentionally avoids speculative technological positioning and unsupported automation narratives.

---

# Development Status

The CRIS use-case architecture framework remains under active methodological and infrastructure development.

Additional operational domains, workflow systems, planning-intelligence layers, and infrastructure coordination architectures may evolve progressively across future repository stages.
