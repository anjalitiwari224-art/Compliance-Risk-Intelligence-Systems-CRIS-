# REGULATORY SCOPE

---

## Overview

The Compliance & Risk Intelligence Systems (CRIS) framework includes a regulatory intelligence layer designed to support:

- regulatory interpretation,
- zoning analysis,
- approval coordination,
- code conflict identification,
- and planning validation workflows.

The framework organizes regulatory information to improve operational clarity and planning coordination across built-environment systems.

---

## Scope of Regulatory Systems

CRIS may structure and interpret information from:

- building bylaws,
- development control regulations,
- zoning frameworks,
- fire safety codes,
- environmental regulations,
- accessibility standards,
- municipal planning systems,
- occupancy classifications,
- circulation requirements,
- and approval workflow structures.

The framework is intended to support operational analysis rather than replace statutory interpretation.

---

## Zoning Intelligence

The zoning layer may assist in organizing and evaluating:

- land-use classifications,
- development permissions,
- density conditions,
- setback requirements,
- height restrictions,
- environmental buffer requirements,
- parking obligations,
- and occupancy limitations.

The objective is to improve clarity during early-stage planning and pre-validation workflows.

---

## Development Control Systems

CRIS may support analysis related to:

- FAR / FSI calculations,
- setback validation,
- circulation compliance,
- occupancy distribution,
- environmental conditions,
- parking systems,
- ventilation requirements,
- and egress conditions.

Development control analysis is treated as an operational planning coordination layer.

---

## Environmental Regulatory Coordination

The framework may assist in organizing environmental planning conditions including:

- flood-risk awareness,
- environmental buffering,
- climatic adaptation considerations,
- daylight access conditions,
- ventilation logic,
- and site-orientation analysis.

Environmental systems are integrated into planning coordination workflows rather than isolated technical reviews.

---

## Fire Safety & Accessibility

CRIS may assist in structuring operational review systems related to:

- fire access pathways,
- egress systems,
- circulation pressure zones,
- emergency movement logic,
- accessibility pathways,
- and occupancy safety coordination.

The framework does not replace certified fire safety review or statutory accessibility compliance approval.

---

## Approval Workflow Coordination

The regulatory layer may support:

- approval sequencing,
- document dependency mapping,
- procedural coordination,
- interdepartmental workflow structuring,
- and bottleneck identification.

The objective is to reduce planning friction and improve coordination clarity before submission stages.

---

## AI-Assisted Regulatory Role

Within CRIS, AI may assist in:

- regulation parsing,
- zoning interpretation,
- clause organization,
- conflict identification,
- approval workflow mapping,
- and structured planning analysis.

AI is not positioned as:

- statutory authority,
- legal certification infrastructure,
- autonomous approval intelligence,
- or professional replacement systems.

---

## Jurisdictional Limitation

Regulatory interpretation within CRIS remains dependent on:

- jurisdiction,
- municipal authority,
- project classification,
- local policy conditions,
- and professional review.

The framework does not guarantee interpretation accuracy across all regions or approval systems.

---

## Operational Position

CRIS treats regulatory systems as:

> operational planning intelligence infrastructure.

The framework prioritizes:

- coordination clarity,
- conflict identification,
- workflow structuring,
- and scalable regulatory intelligence support systems.

---

## Regulatory Limitation

CRIS functions as an AI-assisted regulatory interpretation and planning support framework.

The system does not:

- replace governing authorities,
- issue approvals,
- certify compliance,
- or function as a statutory interpretation authority.

Final interpretation and approval responsibilities remain with licensed professionals and governing institutions.
